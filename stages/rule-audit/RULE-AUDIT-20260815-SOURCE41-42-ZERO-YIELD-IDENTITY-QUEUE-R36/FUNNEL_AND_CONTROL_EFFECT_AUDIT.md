# Source41–42 Funnel and Control-Effect Audit

## Bottom line

The active five-field identity preflight and queue-accountability rule improved **precision and auditability**. The evidence does not show that they caused the zero yield. Recall now fails mainly before a concrete carrier/source route is frozen, plus at the handoff boundary where a legitimate bounded raw can be held for work that the written rules allow Stage 0 to perform independently.

This is not proof that the rule increases total yield. Source41 and Source42 are only two non-paired cycles with different opportunity families, and neither supplied a Stage 0 denominator. The defensible conclusion is narrower: the controls removed known duplicates and false queue claims without a demonstrated related-only false exclusion.

## Did the identity rule improve precision?

Yes, within the observed claim ceiling:

1. The FPMR regression found the known Source19 terminal identity before it could be recycled.
2. The SME/ZA adjacent family negative control remained `RELATED_ONLY`, showing the rule can preserve a neighboring but non-identical family.
3. Source42 lane records show exact/terminal exclusions and related-only retention; no late historical reconciliation was registered.
4. `NO_MATCH` was not promoted to novelty or absence evidence.

What is not established:

- sensitivity on an independently labelled family sample;
- improvement in clean-brief yield;
- improvement in Stage 0 pass rate;
- absence of every possible false negative.

Therefore the correct action is to retain the identity rule and monitor its existing negative-control metrics, not expand its containment semantics.

## Did the queue rule improve accountability?

Yes at final handoff, but it was not lane-native on first pass. The two ambiguous Source42 routes were initially labelled as queues and needed mainline append-only corrections. The final record is honest—zero attempts, no exhausted route, no scientific inference—but first-pass compliance was 0/2 for the only purported queue items.

Source43 already incorporates the missing concreteness condition. It is the running implementation test and must not be changed by this audit. No additional queue-policy delta is justified before its normal completion.

## Where recall now fails

1. **Before raw:** the seed/RQ often names a domain or mechanism class but not one exact versioned carrier, atomic action and estimand. This is visible in Source41 S3–S5 and the corrected Source42 S1/S5 routes.
2. **At raw-to-brief ownership:** Source41 S1 met the written raw contract and froze a finite falsifier, but its handoff treated complete current union, direct paper collision and Q2 calibration as reasons not to route Stage 0. The shared rules explicitly permit these as bounded debt and require Stage 0 to rebuild them independently.
3. **Not demonstrably at global identity:** related-only non-exclusion and the lack of late reconciliation argue against identity overcooling in this sample.

## Competing explanations checked

- **Reasoning/model weakness:** possible, but not identified by these two unpaired cycles; both already used `terra/high`, and there is no paired model contrast.
- **History saturation:** contributes to healthy exact/current-union exclusions, but cannot explain pre-RQ and ambiguous-carrier failures.
- **Scientific hard gates too strict:** not supported. Known direct/current/generic failures should remain fatal. The visible issue is ownership of bounded work, not the hard gate itself.
- **Queue rule too strict:** not supported. A disjunctive project route is not executable or auditable; accepting it would create hidden selection freedom.
