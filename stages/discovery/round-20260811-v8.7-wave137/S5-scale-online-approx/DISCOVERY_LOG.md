This is an N3 search, not an attempt to rename an online controller as a contribution. A plausible analysis question is not evidence that a stable law exists.

## Deep-review outcomes

### Google ClusterData 2019 — `NOT_ADMITTED_UNFROZEN`

Exact object is characterization of per-cell online arrivals, resource requests, placement constraints, and scheduling delay in the released May-2019 Borg traces—not a new scheduler or a pooled cross-provider dataset. The repository explicitly records the 2019 trace semantics and existing Borg longitudinal analysis. It supports natural heterogeneity and time variation, but this review found no first-party result establishing the proposed residual (a scale-normalized, reproducible recourse/queue law across cells after controlling for workload mix). Merely measuring it would not identify whether workload mix, priority, machine heterogeneity, or trace sampling causes it. No complete exploitation action can be frozen without that result. A finite 72-hour check exists (stratified cell/day replay with predeclared holdout cells), so this is neither a scientific DROP nor a proposal.

### CAIDA 100G monthly backbone traces — `NOT_ADMITTED_UNFROZEN`

Exact object is characterization of packet-header traffic dynamics on the documented LA–Dallas 100G link from January 2025 onward (with LA–San Jose only for Apr–Nov 2024), using packets/bytes/rates/link-load denominators. The official catalog records anonymization, one-hour monthly capture, access agreement, public summary metadata, and a 5-second sampler. It does not establish a portable scale/approximation law for online summary rebuild. Link geography transition, payload stripping, anonymization, month/hour and application mix are material confounders. The putative law therefore lacks natural positive evidence, while full trace access is also governed by an AUA. A small metadata/sampler falsifier is finite, but it cannot justify a same-object online-algorithm paper absent a positive law.

### Wikimedia Pageviews — `NOT_ADMITTED_UNFROZEN`

Exact object is the official hourly pageview aggregation stream, with counts under Wikimedia's documented counting logic; it is not generic web caching and does not identify individual requests. The official readme warns that count logic differs across apps, mobile and desktop, which is a direct confounder for any apparent scale law. No first-party source found here establishes a stable law tying corpus scale to approximate-summary/recourse cost after controlling for project, platform, bot filtering and seasonality. A small, version-pinned two-month/hourly split can falsify the hypothesis, but that is a future empirical route rather than evidence supporting a grounded brief.
