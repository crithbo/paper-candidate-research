# Q1/Q2 comparator matrix — PARQUET-PAGE-COSEGMENT

## Candidate shape after current-source subtraction

The viable claim is not “nonuniform Parquet pages” and not “choose a compression codec per page.” Current Arrow already has content-defined nonuniform page boundaries, while Parquet fixes the codec at column-chunk scope. The candidate must be a standard-format, workload-conditioned legal-cut and representation constructor that beats the complete finite current writer/CDC family under a symmetric workload and full cost.

| Liu et al., **Data formats in analytical DBMSs: performance trade-offs and future directions** | VLDB Journal 2025 | Broad empirical study of Parquet/Arrow/ORC encoding, compression and query execution, including format-aware optimizations | Weak format baselines, codec-only claims and narrow microbenchmarks | A constructor over standard Parquet actions with natural full-cost query/write evidence | Q1 journal evaluation/domain anchor |

## Quality verdict

- `TIER_B_Q2_VIABLE`: conditional on legal action refreeze, current CDC parity and a non-product globally budgeted constructor.
- `TIER_A_Q1_POTENTIAL`: not presently supported. It would require reusable theory/cost modeling, multiple workload/data families, and robust native-reader evidence.
- Current readiness does not justify a result claim; it does support a finite Stage A route.

## Structural separation test

The candidate survives only if at least one natural file admits a workload-conditioned legal cut set that is not generated by any preregistered B1/B2 configuration and if joint selection yields a non-dominated full-cost point. If cuts are current CDC boundaries, uniform caps, or a finite existing property choice, the result is configuration tuning rather than N2.
